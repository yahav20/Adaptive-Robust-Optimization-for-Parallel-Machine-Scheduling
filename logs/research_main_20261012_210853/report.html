Findings

  1. Gap frequency. Minimax = MILO (within 0.0001) in 204/212 (96.2%) instances. Gapped instances: mean gap 0.56%, max 0.87% of the MILO value.
  2. No adaptive choice ⇒ no gap. When n − m ≤ 1, only one task is left after the first decision, so the scheduler never chooses again and the leaf LPs cannot anticipate anything: gap in 0/80 (0.0%), anticipation conflicts in 0/80 (0.0%). With n − m ≥ 2: gap in 8/132 (6.1%), conflicts in 126/132 (95.5%).
  3. Gap vs Γ (instances with gap / instances): Γ=0.5: 3/36 (8.3%), Γ=1: 0/36 (0.0%), Γ=1.5: 2/36 (5.6%), Γ=2: 2/36 (5.6%), Γ=2.5: 1/12 (8.3%), Γ=3: 0/36 (0.0%), Γ=4: 0/20 (0.0%).
  4. Conflicts are necessary but not sufficient. Gapped instances with a conflict in the chosen subtree: 7/8 (87.5%). Instances with a conflict that have a gap: 7/101 (6.9%) - most conflicts are on options the scheduler would not pick anyway, so they do not move the MIN.
  5. A cheap exactness certificate. Count conflicts only on the Minimax strategy tree (all options at scheduler nodes, only the chosen child at adversary nodes). With no such conflict the leaf durations merge into one non-anticipative scenario, so Minimax = MILO without solving the MILO. Certified: 94/212 (44.3%); certified instances with a gap (should be 0): 0/94 (0.0%); uncertified instances that are exact anyway (alternative LP optima can resolve a conflict): 110/118 (93.2%). Gapped instances whose conflict lies in another first decision (MILO picks a different first move): 1/8 (12.5%).
  6. Sandwich with one extra LP. Fixing the Minimax adversary strategy and solving one LP with shared durations gives a lower bound LB ≤ MILO ≤ Minimax. LB ≤ MILO + 0.0001 holds in 212/212 (100.0%) (validity check). LB = Minimax (certified exact) in 169/212 (79.7%); certified but gapped (should be 0): 0/169 (0.0%). LB = MILO in 175/212 (82.5%). The fixed strategy cannot be realized with shared durations (LP infeasible, LB = −∞) in 0/212 (0.0%). Otherwise the mean bracket width Minimax − LB is 0.0449 (max 0.9144); LP size ≤ 43 variables, mean time 0.6 ms.
  7. The first decision is robust to the relaxation. Minimax's first decision is also AR-optimal in 210/212 (99.1%) instances; max regret 0.01634.
  8. The adversary does not simply get the worst LP leaf. L* is the worst leaf of the whole tree in 0/212 (0.0%) and of its own first-decision subtree in 103/212 (48.6%). On average 34% of the leaves have a larger LP value than L*; Minimax / worst leaf = 0.839. That ratio is the value of adapting: the scheduler's later MIN choices cut off the worst sequences.
  9. Shape of the locked leaf. Budget fully used (Σu = min(Γ,n)) in 150/212 (70.8%); when it is not, the remaining budget cannot raise the makespan without breaking the event order. ≥1 tight order constraint (a tie of two completion times) in 124/212 (58.5%). Every fractional u is paid for by a tight constraint (#fractional u ≤ 1 + #ties, the LP-vertex count) in 212/212 (100.0%): the adversary pushes tasks to their max (u=1) and uses fractional u only to land exactly on a tie.
  10. Budget off the critical chain. All of Σu on the tasks summed in the makespan in 89/212 (42.0%); on average 22% of the used budget lengthens other tasks, which only matters through the order constraints: it forces the scheduler to start the next task on the worse machine.
  11. LPT is almost the optimal scheduler. Starting the pending task(s) with the largest d0 + d̄ is optimal at 1496/1566 (95.5%) scheduler decisions (chance: 61%). When it is wrong, it loses on average 0.0736 (max 0.3175) of worst-case makespan. Other rules: largest nominal d0 79%, largest deviation d_bar 82%, SPT (smallest d0+d_bar) 29%, random pick (chance) 61%.
